Outcome

AI video tools can add sound effects, but not a musical score. Visuals and music are still made separately, and making them feel like one piece is left to the user.

Synora connects the prompts: what you wrote for the visuals carries into the music, and the system reads the video to match the score to its pacing.

A platform where visuals and music are created as one piece, so users can focus on their vision instead of fitting the two together.

Hans Zimmer

Who is he?

Two-time Oscar-winning film composer (The Lion King, Inception, Interstellar, Dune, Rain Man).

Zimmer often writes music before filming begins. For Interstellar, he composed from a single page Nolan gave him, before seeing any footage.

Some of his beliefs

  • Blending orchestra and electronics
  • Music that exists before the picture
  • Building scores from sounds, not just melody

Each of these shaped a design decision, shown below

Screens Concept

Windows peeking into other people's worlds.

The home page is a grid of windows, each showing a world someone created.
It shows what Synora can do, and invites people to look closer.

Design System

Logo & Symbol

The wordmark combines serif and sans-serif letterforms, the same fusion of acoustic and digital found in Zimmer's work. The symbol takes the O as a camera lens, with the AI spark at its center, looking into the worlds people create.

Typography

Synora sits between film and music, handmade and artificial. Recia, a serif, brings the cinematic, handmade side. Plus Jakarta Sans, a geometric sans, brings structure and a technological feel.

Colors

Early wireframes were black and white, and felt technical and cold. I looked for a color between the warm, handmade side of a live orchestra and the cold, electronic side of synths.
I chose Peach for the warmth while staying soft and calm.

From Critique to Final

“It doesn’t look like an AI platform”

In the final class presentation, the feedback was clear: people expect to enter an AI tool and start creating right away. The early version explained the idea instead of inviting people in.
So the structure changed: a prompt bar moved to the top of every page, navigation moved to a sidebar, and the worlds took the full screen.
